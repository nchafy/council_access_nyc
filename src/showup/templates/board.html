Attending

Board committees admit non-board members of the public.

Public members on board committees

  • Committees are "composed of Board members, and non-Board (public) members".
  • Public members take part in discussion and offer recommendations, but are "not allowed to vote".
  • Most boards require an application and a current resume.
  • Committees are open to the public and, under the New York State Open Meetings Law, must keep full and accurate minutes.
  • Committees often meet at the board office listed above, and the full board often does not. Several boards publish that split — one lists "Board Office" against every committee and "varies" against the full board; others meet elsewhere or on video.

Board membership

  • Each board has up to 50 unsalaried members, and half are nominated by the City Council members whose districts cover it.
  • Members are appointed by the Borough President, whose office processes applications at various times of the year.
  • Members must "reside, work, or have some other significant interest in the community".
  • Applications go to the {{ board.borough_name }} Borough President: {% if bp_url %}{{ m.link(bp_url, bp_url.replace("https://", "").rstrip("/")) }}{% else %}nyc.gov/communityboards{% endif %}

What this board can and cannot do

  • Zoning: "Applications for a change in or variance from the zoning resolution must come before the board for review." Boards must also be consulted on the siting of most municipal facilities.
  • Budget: boards assess local needs and meet with City agencies to make recommendations in the City's budget process.
  • The limit, stated by the City: boards "do not have the ability to order any City agency or official to perform any task".
  • Boards are "autonomous City agencies and members are City officers" — not a neighbourhood association.
