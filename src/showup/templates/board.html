How to get involved with this board

The route most people do not know about: board committees admit non-board members of the public. You can join the discussion and make recommendations without being appointed to anything — you just cannot vote.

Join a committee as a public member

  • Committees are "composed of Board members, and non-Board (public) members".
  • As a public member you take part in discussion and offer recommendations, but are "not allowed to vote".
  • Most boards ask for an application and a current resume — contact the board office above.
  • Committees are open to the public and, under the New York State Open Meetings Law, must keep full and accurate minutes.
  • Committees often meet at the board office listed above, and the full board often does not. Several boards publish exactly that split — one lists "Board Office" against every committee and "varies" against the full board. Others meet elsewhere or on video, and at least one tells readers to ring before assuming. The office address is the one we can give you; the room for a specific meeting is on the board's own site.

Apply to be a board member

  • Each board has up to 50 unsalaried members, and half are nominated by the City Council members whose districts cover it — which is why the council districts listed above matter.
  • Members are appointed by the Borough President, whose office processes applications at various times of the year.
  • You must "reside, work, or have some other significant interest in the community".
  • Apply through the {{ board.borough_name }} Borough President: {% if bp_url %}{{ m.link(bp_url, bp_url.replace("https://", "").rstrip("/")) }}{% else %}see nyc.gov/communityboards{% endif %}

What this board can and cannot do

  • Zoning: "Applications for a change in or variance from the zoning resolution must come before the board for review." Boards must also be consulted on the siting of most municipal facilities.
  • Budget: boards assess local needs and meet with City agencies to make recommendations in the City's budget process.
  • The limit, stated plainly by the City: boards "do not have the ability to order any City agency or official to perform any task". They advise, and are often persuasive.
  • Boards are "autonomous City agencies and members are City officers" — not a neighbourhood association.

Things the City does not publish

We could not find an official source for how long a board term lasts, any minimum age to serve, how often the full board must meet, or whether and how the public may speak at a full board meeting. Those vary by board and we will not guess. Ask the board office directly — its phone and email are above.
