How to be heard

Only Council Members can introduce legislation. There is no public petition route onto the agenda, so the levers below are testimony and your member.

Speak at a hearing

  • In person: no pre-registration. Turn up and sign up at the hearing.
  • Remotely, by Zoom web or phone: register in advance on the Register to Testify form.
  • For Zoning and Franchises, or Landmarks, Public Sitings and Dispositions, use the Land Use page instead.

File written testimony

  • Accepted up to 72 hours after the hearing is adjourned — so you can still act after a hearing you missed.
  • DOC, DOCX or PDF, maximum 10 MB, through the same form.
  • Pre-recorded testimony is not played. Audio or video links need a transcript too.
  • Whatever you submit becomes part of the permanent public record.

Two things nobody publishes

The Council does not publish a registration cut-off time before a hearing, or a per-speaker time limit. We will not guess at either. Chairs usually announce a limit at the start. To confirm for a specific hearing, ask the hearings office: hearings@council.nyc.gov or 212-482-4219.

What happens after you testify

The committee may amend the bill, then votes. If it passes by majority it goes to the full Council, then to the Mayor, who has 30 days to sign it, veto it, or do nothing — in which case it becomes law anyway. The Council can override a veto with a two-thirds vote.
